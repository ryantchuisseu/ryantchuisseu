In this village, nobody has ever slept. Except me.

My name is Milo, and I live in Lantern Hollow. Every evening, all two hundred villagers gather in the square and walk in slow circles around the Great Lamp. Nobody sits. Nobody lies down. Nobody closes their eyes.

The elders say there is one rule. Never fall asleep, because the moon is hungry. If you sleep, it steals your dreams, and you wake up empty. We call those people the Hollows. They stand in the street, smiling at nothing. There are seven of them, and nobody goes near them.

But here is my secret. I sleep every single night. I hide in the old windmill, and every night I dream of a field of floating lanterns. And every morning, I wake up fine. Nothing stolen. Nothing empty.

I never told anyone, not even my little sister, Ava. But I noticed something strange. The Great Lamp always burns brightest at dawn, right after I wake up.

So one night, I stayed awake.

At midnight, the Great Lamp began to flicker. In a hundred years, it had never flickered once. Soon it was almost dark. And then I heard it. A deep, low humming, coming from the moon.

Silver light poured down over the square. It was searching. And the seven Hollows turned their heads, all at the same time, and started walking toward me.

I tried to run, but the elders blocked every street. Elder Rowan held up his hands and said, "Milo, please. Go to sleep. The lamp is dying."

That's when I understood. The lamp was never burning on oil. It burned on dreams. And in a village where nobody sleeps, there was only one dreamer left. Me.

The elders never walked in circles to hide from the moon. They walked to keep everyone awake, so the lamp would have only one dreamer to feed on.

I looked at the Hollows and asked who they were. Elder Rowan stared at the ground and said, "They were dreamers too."

Then my sister, Ava, stepped out of the crowd, took my hand, and whispered, "Milo, you were never the first dreamer. You are the last one. And tonight, the lamp needs more than a dream."

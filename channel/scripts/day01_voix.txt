In this village, nobody has ever slept. Except me.

My name is Milo, and I live in Lantern Hollow. Here, the night belongs to the walkers. Every evening, when the sun goes down, all two hundred villagers gather in the square and walk in slow circles around the Great Lamp. Nobody sits. Nobody lies down. Nobody closes their eyes.

The elders say there is one rule. Never fall asleep, because the moon is hungry. If you sleep, it steals your dreams, and you wake up empty. We call those people the Hollows. They stand in the street with open eyes, smiling at nothing. There are seven of them in our village, and nobody ever goes near them.

But here is my secret. I sleep every single night. I hide in the old windmill for two hours after midnight, and every night I have the same dream. A field of floating lanterns, and one silver door. And every morning, I wake up fine. Nothing stolen. Nothing empty.

I never told anyone, not even my little sister, Ava. But I did notice one strange thing. The Great Lamp always burns brightest at dawn, right after I wake up.

So one night, I wondered what would happen if I did not sleep. I stayed awake and walked with everyone else.

At midnight, the Great Lamp began to flicker. The whole village stopped. In a hundred years, that lamp had never flickered once. By two o'clock, it was almost dark. People started to panic. And then I heard it. A deep, low humming, coming from the moon.

Silver light poured down over the square. It was searching. And the seven Hollows turned their heads, all at the same time, and started walking toward me.

I tried to run, but the elders blocked every street. The oldest one, Elder Rowan, held up his hands and said, "Milo, please. Go to sleep."

I told him I was not tired. He said, "Then close your eyes anyway. The lamp is dying."

That's when I understood. The lamp was never burning on oil. It burned on dreams. And in a village where nobody sleeps, there was only one dreamer left. Me.

The elders never walked in circles to protect everyone from the moon. They walked to keep everyone else awake, so the lamp would have only one dreamer to feed on.

I looked at the Hollows, still smiling at nothing, and I asked who they were. Elder Rowan stared at the ground and said, "They were dreamers too."

Then my sister, Ava, stepped out of the crowd, took my hand, and whispered, "Milo, you were never the first dreamer. You are the last one. And tonight, the lamp needs more than a dream."
